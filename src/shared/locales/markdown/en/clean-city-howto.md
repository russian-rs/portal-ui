# Clean City (core program)

### Program Description

The Clean City program is an initiative for those who want to make their surroundings cleaner and more pleasant.
The program aims to support the environmental situation in Serbian cities through regular cleanups of public spaces and various eco-events that promote an eco-friendly lifestyle. Thanks to the joint efforts of participants, we create a cozy and safe environment for ourselves, our children, and everyone around us.

### Cleanup Recommendations for Clean City Volunteers

Safety during cleanup:

- Before starting, wear gloves and comfortable closed-toe shoes.
- Be careful when handling broken glass, rusty metal, and other hazardous items.
- When working near roads or bodies of water, take precautions and avoid dangerous areas.
- In hot weather, remember to drink water and take breaks to avoid overheating.
- It is strongly discouraged to collect used medical items/bird carcasses and anything potentially hazardous to health.

Waste collection and disposal:

- Use sturdy bags and tie them so that the trash is not scattered by the wind.
- After cleanup, leave the bags in designated areas for municipal services to pick up. If you have collected too much trash in a hard-to-reach place and cannot transport it to a bin, inform the program curator to submit a request to the city authorities.
- If possible, separate waste: collect plastic, glass, and paper separately for recycling.

Following these rules will make the cleanup process effective, safe, and convenient for all program participants. A clean city is everyone’s responsibility!

Required weekly volunteer effort

All volunteers participate in the program under the Volunteer Law ([Službeni glasnik RS, No. 36/2010](https://russian.rs/laws/zakon-o-volonterstve/)). Each volunteer must devote at least 10 hours per week to volunteer activities. You choose how to allocate this time:

- Do a large-area cleanup in one day (e.g., in a park or along a specific street).
- Split the cleanup across several days in different places (e.g., near your home or at a playground).
- Other options agreed with the curator (e.g., maintaining cleanliness of a specific area).

It is important that the total for the week amounts to at least 10 hours of volunteer activity.

If you cannot purchase bags, gloves, or other supplies yourself, the organization is ready to provide them. Contact the curator in advance to arrange pickup. If you purchase consumables at your own expense, you may, no more than once every two weeks, include one hour in your weekly report for purchasing supplies.
If you have meetings during the week with the Clean City program curator or other organizational meetings within the activities of the Diaspora, you may include the time spent on these meetings in the weekly report.
In addition to direct cleanup, additional tasks are periodically distributed, including monitoring illegal dumpsites, searching for information about eco-friendly organizations and events in Serbia, and so on. Additional tasks are competitive and are offered in the general Clean City program chat. If you have additional ideas for developing the direction, write to the Clean City curator Anton on Telegram @Giktom.

Volunteer responsibilities in the Clean City program — 6 steps

1. Choosing a cleanup location: The volunteer independently chooses the area to clean in any convenient location (nearby yard, cluttered public space, another city). This can be a yard, park, square, boulevard, embankment, roadside along a street, playgrounds, or other public areas.

Not recommended: cleaning on the territory of commercial organizations (for example, cleaning around a nightclub or the Microsoft building). It is prohibited to clean areas where access is restricted by the owner or authorities (e.g., fenced construction site).

2. Defining cleanup boundaries: Clearly outline the boundaries of the selected area to plan the work effectively and assess the scope of tasks (a map screenshot with a marked cleaned area will be needed in the next step).

3. Estimating the amount and type of waste: Before starting, visually assess the volume and types of waste. If separate collection is planned, prepare appropriate bags for different types of waste (plastic, glass, paper, etc.).

4. If you discover an illegal dumpsite that cannot physically be cleaned up on your own (trash in water, discarded furniture, etc.), fill out the internal Clean City monitoring form at: https://forms.gle/Qa8nxn4KGCTd8WKh7. One found dumpsite may be counted in the weekly report as you spent time finding it, describing the situation on-site, taking photos, and filling out the form.

5. Photographing the process: Be sure to take BEFORE and AFTER photos. Pay attention to the shooting angle; BEFORE and AFTER photos are strongly recommended to be taken from the same point. Take a wide shot of the area before work, the collected bags, and the final result after cleaning. Photos serve as proof of work performed.

6. Weekly reporting: Every week the volunteer must submit a report on the work done on the volunteer portal.

To fill out the weekly report, you need to:

1. Log in to the volunteer portal.

2. On the left menu panel, select “New report.”

3. Fill in all required fields of the form and upload the necessary photos to the cloud at: https://files.russian.rs/f/13839. The requirements for the folder structure in the cloud are described below.

Required fields to fill out in the volunteer portal report:

1. Title: enter the task title in the format:
   - “Cleanup of [specify exact place].” (If there were several places during the week, create a separate task for each.)
   - Example: “Cleanup of Kalemegdan Park,” “Cleanup of Kneza Mihaila Street.”

2. Description: briefly describe what exactly you did. Specify what you cleaned (for example: plastic, paper, glass, leaves, etc.), whether separate waste collection was used. The description must include the collected volume of waste in liters (use the bag volume as a reference).
   - Example: “Cleanup of trash and dry leaves near the Russian school. Collected 360 liters.” “Collected plastic, glass, and paper; swept paths; removed leftover branches. Used separate collection. Collected 720 liters.”

3. Result: in this field, paste a link to the cloud where your photos have already been uploaded: https://files.russian.rs/f/13839. In the folder with your first and last name, create a separate folder for each location with photos and add photos to each of these folders. For each location, add a link specifically to that location’s folder to the “Result” field — you need to create a link (details shown in the screenshot).

![1](/resources/cleaning/1.png)

- Required photos:
  - Photo before cleanup (show polluted areas).
  - Photo after cleanup (from the same angle to show the difference).
  - Photo of collected trash bags.
  - Screenshot of Google Maps with the cleaned area highlighted.
  - When taking photos, make sure no passers-by, bystanders, or children are in the frame. This is necessary to comply with privacy and safety rules. If it is impossible to avoid people in the frame, choose another angle or take the photo when no one is around.
- Incorrect photos:
  - Only a photo of a clean place without proof of cleanup.
  - A photo of a small area with a large amount of claimed work time.
  - Photos taken at night.
  - Reusing old photos.

If you have not been granted access to the cloud: write to the Clean City program curator Anton on Telegram @Giktom.

4. Number of hours: indicate the actual amount of time you spent on the cleanup. If cleanup was carried out in several areas, create separate tasks for each area with the time indicated. If you performed other tasks within the Clean City program, participated in organizational meetings, or purchased consumables yourself, also include the time spent on this in the report.

5. Date: specify the actual cleanup date.

6. Client: fill in only if the cleanup was at someone’s request.

7. Files: photos are NOT attached to the reports.

8. After filling in all the fields, click “Submit.”
